# Customer Technical Deliverable

Produce a decision-ready artifact whose claims can be traced back to reliable evidence.

## Frame the artifact

- Identify the audience, decision or action the artifact should support, required language, delivery format, and deadline from available context.
- Inspect existing templates, prior materials, terminology, and repository instructions before choosing a new structure or visual style.
- Separate verified facts, calculations, assumptions, estimates, open questions, and recommendations. Never present an inference as a sourced fact.
- For current product, price, law, specification, or service information, verify against authoritative current sources and record the source and access date where appropriate.

## Build the content

- Lead with the conclusion or decision needed, then provide supporting evidence and details.
- Use the customer's terminology consistently. Define unavoidable technical terms in plain language.
- Make quantities auditable: state units, periods, inclusions, exclusions, formulas, rounding, and uncertainty.
- Use tables for exact comparisons, diagrams for relationships or flows, and prose for interpretation. Add a visual only when it improves understanding.
- Preserve the requested artifact's natural language and tone; do not apply conversational bilingual style to the deliverable itself.

## Validate the artifact

- Cross-check names, dates, totals, links, figure labels, citations, and conclusions against the source material.
- Check that every recommendation follows from the presented evidence and that limitations or unresolved items are visible.
- For DOCX, PPTX, XLSX, or PDF output, render the final artifact and visually inspect every page or slide for clipping, overflow, illegible text, broken characters, and inconsistent layout.
- Deliver the editable source together with the final distribution format when the workflow calls for both.
